# Combat Design — Frontage v1 (IMPLEMENTED)

**Status:** IMPLEMENTED. Current app version comes from `package.json`; wire protocol from `src/lib/game/net/protocol.ts`.  
**Proposal version:** 1.0 — 2026-11-20  
**Replaces:** EXP-001 attack semantics ([EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md))  
**Scope:** attack/combat rules only. Economy beyond supply, progression, networking, and UI polish are out of scope.

> **Approved decisions:** 51 states; one supply resource; durable standing fronts with integer strength;
> simultaneous mutual attrition; cancellation with proportional refunds; instant victory at 70% land;
> no match time limit.
>
> The implemented model is **Model B — Frontage v1**. It keeps the proven growth-layer,
> least-resistance, district, and integrity machinery and replaces the attack-order economy, front
> lifetime, and order-stripping clash with supply, strength, attrition, idle expiry, and refunds.
>
> The rest of this document retains the longer design rationale and migration notes. Section 8 is now a
> historical decision list; the approved subset is the paragraph above.

---

## 1. Why the experiment must end

EXP-001 proved the pipeline, not the game. It is deterministic and fast under absurd load:

- 51 states, dozens of simultaneous fronts, 32 × 32 dirty-tile uploads and chunked full resync stay interactive.
- Tick cost at 51 states is roughly 1.6–2.4 ms depending on growth layer, full checksum ~17 ms on demand.
- Mutual-front stripping, annexation, districts, and auras all pass determinism and invariant tests.

Those are engineering results. The attack rules themselves have five structural problems that tuning
cannot fix:

1. **Orders are a free timer, not a cost.** Each launch spends one inventory order; orders arrive
   automatically every 100 ticks (`ATTACK_ORDER_GAIN_TICKS`). Attacks are therefore limited by
   wall-clock patience, not by land, investment, or risk. There is no link between how much of the map
   a player holds and how much war they can wage.
2. **Defense has no positional price.** A defender with spare inventory orders strips an incoming
   front at contact by spending orders that would otherwise start attacks. Terrain and structures slow
   a front, but a defender never pays supply, land, or time to hold a line. The cheapest defense is
   always "have an order bank".
3. **A click can engulf an arbitrary region.** One front flood-fills any connected target territory
   until its lifetime (`50 + 10 × (initial orders − 1)` ticks) or budget runs out. A single click can
   represent an entire war; there is no frontage, supply line, or commitment.
4. **Fronts are throwaway and churny.** They expire on a short fixed timer, die instantly on one
   zero-capture tick, and cannot be reinforced or cancelled. Click tempo, not strategy, dominates.
5. **Pacing is unshapable.** EXPERIMENTS records map saturation in roughly 10 seconds when pressure is
   unthrottled and a near-even patchwork leaderboard. There is no mechanism that makes late game
   resolve decisively without a hard fiat.

**What must be preserved by any replacement:** simulation purity, fixed 10 Hz integer ticks, stable
ordering, growth layers as candidate _selectors only_, edge-connected captures, `claimedBy` provenance,
State-anchored annexation, bounded districts, derived auras, and the existing checksum/replay contract.

## 2. The three viable models

### 2.1 Model A — Order + Flood v2 (continue and polish)

Keep the current model and patch its worst edges:

- Add a 10-tick grace instead of instant zero-capture death.
- ~~Fix the unaffordable-candidate requeue~~ — fixed in v0.24 (ADR-031).
- Make contact loss symmetric so a defender's order bank is not free defense.
- Reserve pending attack commands against spendable orders.
- Cap captures per front per tick or total captures per front.
- Add a cancel command with a partial order refund.
- Scale launch cost / capture cost with attacker land share to slow snowballing.

**Verdict:** fastest to ship and lowest migration risk, but it still cannot answer "why does holding
land make me stronger?" or "why does a defender pay for a border?" Any real answer to those questions
converges on Model B. Recommended only as the fallback/benchmark mode, not as the final model.

### 2.2 Model B — Frontage v1 (supply-fed standing fronts) — RECOMMENDED

Keep flood-fill geometry and least-resistance capture, but:

- Replace the attack-order inventory with a **supply** resource generated by territory and structures.
- Launching a front **buys a durable integer strength pool**; the front spends strength on every
  capture and on contact attrition.
- Fronts have no short fixed lifetime; they die when strength, land connection, or patience runs out.
- Mutual fronts **trade strength simultaneously**; defense is terrain + structures + counter-fronts,
  never a free order bank.
- Land share converts into supply income, which converts into more front strength. Winning means
  converting territory into war materiel efficiently.

**Verdict:** recommended. Reuses ~80% of the current attack pipeline, is fully integer, and directly
fixes the five problems above. Bots and the HUD need rework, not the renderer or map.

### 2.3 Model C — Army stacks (classic territory-war units)

Land produces troops; players move integer army stacks between owned cells; stack-vs-stack combat and
per-cell garrisons decide captures. This is the OpenFront-like direction and gives the clearest front
lines and tactical micro.

**Verdict:** strongest long-term fantasy, but it is a new simulation subsystem: movement paths, stack
storage/rendering, per-cell combat, pathing determinism, bot planning, and a new UI. Too large for the
next milestone. Keep as a possible v2 of Model B; do not start from the current flood-fill code.

### 2.4 Comparison

| Criterion                           | A — Order + Flood v2      | **B — Frontage v1 (recommended)** | C — Army stacks       |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Migration cost                      | Lowest                    | Medium                            | High / new subsystem  |
| Keeps current attack machinery      | Yes                       | Yes (capture, clash replaced)     | No (rewritten)        |
| Land → military power link          | None (timer only)         | Direct (supply income)            | Direct (troops)       |
| Defender pays for defense           | No (order bank)           | Yes (strength/clash)              | Yes (units)           |
| Reads as a front line at world zoom | Weak                      | Good                              | Best                  |
| Match-length controllability        | Weak                      | Strong (income + strength math)   | Strong but complex    |
| Determinism risk                    | Very low                  | Low (integers only)               | High (movement/order) |
| Recommended                         | Fallback / benchmark mode | **Ship this**                     | Park for later        |

## 3. Recommended model: Frontage v1 — exact rules

All values are integers. New tuning names are proposals; they live in `constants.ts` and are part of
`COMBAT_RULES_VERSION`.

### 3.1 New player state

Each player gets:

- `supply` — integer stock, `0..supplyCap`.
- `supplyAccrual` — integer remainder, `0..9` (same continuous-accrual pattern as gold).
- `holdTicks` — integer, for the dominance victory (§3.8). Not player-visible until close to winning.

Derived per tick from live territory and structures (same derived-stat style as `income` and
`defenseAt` — never a mutable per-cell array):

```text
supplyCap(player)    = 120 + floor(territory / 1000) + 80 × WarAcademyStacks   // no maximum
supplyIncome(player) =  3  + floor(territory / 3000) +  1 × CommandPostStacks   // per second, no cap
```

- Start: `supply = 90` for every player (human and bots). Base cap at spawn (~800 land cells) is 120,
  so a player can bank three base launches immediately.
- Accrual runs every tick, before commands:

```text
supplyAccrual += supplyIncome
gained         = floor(supplyAccrual / 10)        // 10 ticks = 1 second
supplyAccrual -= gained × 10
supply         = min(supplyCap, supply + gained)  // whole units above cap are lost
```

- The remainder is authoritative checksum state. There is no float and no wall clock.
- The human HUD's ORDERS readout is replaced by **SUPPLY / CAP**, active FRONTS, a supply-per-second number, and CANCEL LAST.
- Supply is the single resource and also pays for cards and upgrades.

### 3.2 Attack initiation

A click is valid under the same geometry rules as today (§4.4 of [SPEC.md](SPEC.md)):

- match not ended, attacker alive;
- clicked cell is land and not owned by the attacker;
- at least one target-owned cell within `ATTACK_SEARCH_RADIUS` (34) touches attacker territory;
- `supply >= launchCost`. There is no explicit front cap; supply and 20-tick idle expiry bound fronts.

On the next tick the command is applied in `(sequence, playerId)` order:

```text
effectiveLevels = max(0, attacker Barracks stacks − defender Jammer stacks)
logTier         = 31 - clz32(effectiveLevels + 1)        // floor(log2(levels + 1))
launchCost      = 30 + 40 × effectiveLevels              // no cap
strength        = 1000 + 200 × effectiveLevels           // no cap
powerPerTick    = 30 + 15 × logTier
pierce          = logTier
supply         -= launchCost
```

- The front starts with `strength` points and the same border-contact seed set, anchor, and growth
  mode as today.
- **Repeated clicks always create separate fronts.** There is no click-to-reinforce, matching the
  current ADR-024 decision. Reinforcement via a new command is deliberately out of scope for v1.
- The order inventory, order cap, and 10-second order grant are removed. Command Post and War Academy
  are repurposed (§3.5).

**Worked example — base front vs neutral lowland.** `effectiveLevels = 0`, launch cost 30, strength 1000,
power 30/tick, pierce 0. Lowland neutral defense = `1 + 0 + 0 = 1`; occupied defense = 2. The front
spends 30 points/tick and can capture at least `1000 / 2 = 500` occupied/lowland pixels, spread over
as many ticks as its frontier allows (minimum 34 ticks at full spend). It is durable rather than time-boxed.

### 3.3 Front processing per tick

Fronts are processed in ascending creation-id order (unchanged). For each live front:

```text
if attacker dead                    -> remove
if tick - createdAt >= 300          -> remove (hard hygiene cap only)
if strength <= 0                    -> remove
budget = min(powerPerTick, strength)

while budget > 0 and strength > 0:
    sample up to 10 candidates from the growth layer in growth order
    for each sampled candidate, in order:
        skip if not land or not owned by defenderId        (no point cost)
        skip if it no longer shares a land edge with the attacker
        if it touches a cell claimedBy an opposing mutual front:
            resolve attrition for the pair (see 3.6), pause this front for the tick
            requeue the whole sample, break
        cost = max(1, defense(cell) − pierce)               (see 3.4)
    if no valid candidate:
        if frontier empty -> remove front
        else break (all sampled cells were stale/disconnected)
    choose the cheapest valid sampled cell; ties keep sample order
    requeue every unchosen sampled cell in reverse sample order
    if chosen cost > remaining budget:
        requeue the chosen cell too and break the tick   (fixes the current code bug, Appendix B item 4)
    claim the cell, strength -= cost, budget -= cost, captures += 1
    push W/E/N/S neighbours owned by defenderId

if no capture and no attrition hold this tick:
    idleTicks += 1
    if idleTicks >= 20 -> remove front (walled in or disconnected; no refund)
else:
    idleTicks = 0
```

- **Front strength is the durable pool.** `powerPerTick` is only the per-tick spending cap, so a front
  cannot dump its whole strength in one tick.
- Strength is spent on captures. There is no separate "lifetime points" budget.
- The 300-tick cap is hygiene/UI only; strength or idle normally ends a front first.
- A front removed for any cause is removed before later fronts process, so capture order stays
  deterministic.
- A front that meets more than one opposing front in a tick exchanges with the first one it
  encounters in candidate order and pauses for the rest of the tick; the pair is marked resolved, so
  the other front does not double-dip that tick.

### 3.4 Defense and capture cost

Defense is unchanged from the implemented model and remains fully derived:

```text
defense(cell) = 1 + elevationTier + (owner != 0 ? 1 : 0) + friendlyAuraDefense
captureCost   = max(1, defense − logTier)             // per capture, paid from strength
```

- `elevationTier` is the versioned 6-tier map data (`MAP_VERSION` unchanged).
- State aura: +20 within 16 px (max-stacking, friendly-owned land only).
- Fortification: +1 per stack over every cell of its own district (additive, friendly-only).
- Jammer: global enemy suppression, −1 effective Barracks level per stack; never alters pixel defense.
- Districts, structure spacing (8 px), stacking, and razing rules are unchanged.
- Barracks is the offensive scaler: each level adds +200 strength and +40 launch cost; each log2 tier
  adds +15 power and +1 piercing. Scaling never caps
  cost (see 3.6).
- Example: State-aura highland occupied pixel with `logTier = 0` costs `max(1, 1+3+1+20) = 25`
  strength; a base front of 1000 strength can take 60 such pixels — a real, readable price.
- Because strength is finite, a high-defense wall can outlast a front. This is intentional; whether
  Fortification stacking needs a cap or a siege rule is an open decision (§8).

### 3.5 Structures and cards under Frontage v1

The permanent seven-card hand; costs live in [REFERENCE.md](REFERENCE.md):

| Card          | Implemented effect                                                       |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Artillery     | Active: 300 supply/level, ±36 px spread, 64 px district, State-immune.   |
| Barracks      | +1 effective level; +200 strength/+40 cost per level, log2 power/pierce. |
| Fortification | +1 defense per stack across its district.                                |
| Jammer        | Global −1 effective enemy Barracks level per stack; no pixel defense.    |
| Command Post  | +1 supply per second per stack.                                          |
| War Academy   | +80 supply cap per stack; no global maximum.                             |
| Port          | Shore-only naval landing anchor; protects sea-linked land components.    |

- `supply-income`, `barracks-power`, and `barracks-suppression` are derived from live stacks via the cache.
- Capturing a Command Post or War Academy removes its supply rate/cap immediately; current supply is
  clamped to the new cap on the next accrual tick.
- Structure districts, auras, razing, and annexation are untouched.

### 3.6 Attrition between fronts

Contact detection stays the same: a candidate is adjacent (4-neighbour) to a cell whose
`claimedBy` id belongs to an active front whose attacker/defender relationship is the reverse of the
current front's.

At contact, resolve **one exchange per unordered front pair per tick**:

```text
lossToA = min(A.strength, 100 + 50 × B.logTier)
lossToB = min(B.strength, 100 + 50 × A.logTier)
apply both simultaneously using pre-exchange strengths
mark both fronts contactHeldThisTick = true
if A.strength == 0 -> remove A after exchange
if B.strength == 0 -> remove B after exchange
```

- Each side loses strength based on the **opponent's** Barracks investment (100 to 350 per contact
  tick), so barracks advantage compounds: it increases your damage and your pool.
- Neither front captures during a contact-held tick; the contact line is the border.
- A base-vs-base contact kills both fronts in 10 exchange ticks (1000 / 100) if neither spends
  strength elsewhere. A high-level Barracks front (for example 2600 strength and 250 contact damage)
  beats a base front in roughly 4 exchanges and survives with most of its pool. That asymmetry is the point.
- A defender with no counter-front is not helpless: it pays no attrition, but the attacker pays
  capture cost from its own finite strength. Terrain, State auras, and Fortifications are the passive
  defense.
- Third-party attacks and attacks against a common target are unaffected (unchanged rule).
- No inventory orders are spent; the "defender order bank" defense is deleted.

### 3.7 Expiry, cancellation, refunds

- A front ends when: strength reaches 0, it has 20 consecutive non-contact ticks with zero captures,
  the attacker is eliminated, its frontier is exhausted, or the 300-tick hygiene cap is reached.
- **No refunds.** Launch supply is spent; remaining strength is materiel already committed and is
  lost when the front ends. This is explicit, simple, and prevents "launch, refund, repeat" exploits.
- **No cancel command in v1.** A front is a commitment. If playtesting demands disengagement, add a
  cancel that refunds **0** supply and simply stops the front; do not add a partial refund without a
  new decision.
- Captures on the tick that reduces strength to 0 are allowed; the front is removed afterwards.

### 3.8 Victory and match length

Three end conditions, checked after eliminations each tick, in this order:

1. **Last state standing** (existing rule): zero territory eliminates a state; last alive wins.
2. **Dominance hold:** a player whose `territory × 10000 >= landCount × 6000` (60% of all land) for
   `600` consecutive ticks (60 s) wins. `holdTicks` increments/resets per player; the threshold is a
   strict majority, so at most one player can satisfy it.
3. **Time limit:** at tick `9000` (15 minutes), the winner is the alive player with the highest
   territory; tie-break by total live structure stacks, then by lowest player id. Result screen shows
   the end cause (`elimination` | `dominance` | `timeout`).

- Target match length for the shipping configuration — **8 human seats + bots on the standard map**:
  median **8–12 minutes**, p90 ≤ 15 minutes, and fewer than 10% of matches decided by timeout.
- The 51-state stress configuration should keep last-alive-only rules and remain a load-test/benchmark
  mode behind a rules flag (it is not a shipping match shape).
- Capturing a State structure keeps the current ADR-012 consequence: territory survives but loses
  annexation protection. Whether capital capture should eliminate/neutralize a state is an open
  decision (§8).

### 3.9 Attack failure reasons

Replace the `no-orders` reason with `no-supply`; add `front-cap`. Full list:
`ended`, `eliminated`, `ocean`, `own-territory`, `no-shared-border`, `no-supply`, `front-cap`,
`invalid`. Editor/UI copy should name the actual shortfall (e.g. "Need 60 supply, have 42." or
"8 fronts active — let one finish.").

### 3.10 Bots

Bots use exactly the same supply, front-cap, strength, and attrition rules. Only decisions change:

- `runBotDecisions` requires the full `launchCost` in supply and a free front slot instead of an
  order. Attack chance is derived from target utility, aggression, supply pressure, and reserves, not
  a flat aggression roll.
- `estimatePlayerStrength` is `floor(territory / 4) + total structure levels × 300 + supply × 2`.
- Economy actions are utility-scored builds/upgrades using per-type affinity, novelty and repeat
  penalties, threat response, income/cap pressure, and efficiency. A strategic reserve funds the
  highest-affinity missing type (or War Academy cap growth when the cap is the bottleneck), and
  expansion becomes the priority when no legal build site exists.
- Each bot compares income and estimated strength with bordering neighbours. A higher neighbour
  income raises Command Post/War Academy priority and reduces attack appetite; a stronger neighbour
  raises Fortification utility, desired copies/levels, and a capped deterrence threat contribution.
- `scoreBotTarget` additionally penalizes targets with large total active front strength
  (caution/conciliation) and rewards targets already under front pressure (opportunism). A stronger
  neighbour below 25% supply (or below its counterattack cost) is a supply opportunity: its
  caution/conciliation penalty is reduced and it gains score and attack-chance bonuses. Grievances
  are unchanged.
- No new RNG: all bot decisions remain `hash32`/existing shared-PRNG draws with the same ordering
  discipline. Utility/reserve comparisons are integer.

## 4. Determinism and versioning

### 4.1 Determinism rules

1. All new quantities are integers. No floats, no wall clock, no new RNG stream.
2. Supply accrual is integer remainder math, identical in shape to gold and Command Post accrual.
3. Front processing is in creation-id order; candidate order is growth-layer order; tie-breaks are
   integer and explicit.
4. Attrition is **simultaneous**: both losses are computed from the same pre-exchange strengths and
   then applied. Resolution order is the processing order of the lower front id; the pair is marked
   resolved for the tick, so a second contact cannot double-dip.
5. The unaffordable-candidate path must requeue **all** sampled cells (including the cheapest) and end
   the tick. This matches SPEC §4.3.12, ARCHITECTURE, and DETERMINISM; the current code does not, and
   the fix is a prerequisite (Appendix B item 4).
6. Per-front state (`strength`, `effectiveLevels`, `logTier`, `power`, `idleTicks`, contact flag, growth-layer
   frontier/PRNG state) is checksummed. Structurally removed fields (`orders`, `initialOrders`,
   `lifetimeTicks`) are removed from the new checksum path.
7. Bots must not use the shared RNG for supply decisions; supply affordability is a comparison.

### 4.2 Snapshot and wire additions

`GameSnapshot` gains `supply`, `supplyCap`, `supplyIncome`, `combatRules`. `PublicAttack` gains
`strength`, `power`, `effectiveLevels`, `logTier`, `idleTicks`. `PublicPlayer` may gain supply only in the
human snapshot, or for all players if the server wants a readable scoreboard — open decision.

### 4.3 Versioning

- Add `COMBAT_RULES_VERSION = 1` in `constants.ts`; include it in the snapshot, match metadata, replay
  JSON, and the protocol `hello`. Old stress checksums are keyed by the rules version.
- Bump `PROTOCOL_VERSION` from its value at implementation time (30 when this proposal was written;
  concurrent non-combat work may advance it first), because attack and player payloads change.
- `MAP_VERSION` stays 4: no map or elevation change.
- Add `SimulationOptions.combatRules: 'stress' | 'frontage'`. Default stays `'stress'` until the
  change is approved and the validation gates pass; then flip the default to `'frontage'` and keep
  `'stress'` for the 51-state benchmark. On a server this is authoritative match configuration, never
  a client-side switch.
- Replays must record `combatRules`, `combatRulesVersion`, `protocolVersion`, and `mapVersion`. Changing
  any proposed constant is a rules-version bump, not a silent balance patch.

## 5. Migration plan from the current code

Each phase should land as a separate reviewed commit with green `pnpm check`, `pnpm test`, and
`pnpm build`. Do not combine the rules flip with unrelated balance work.

**Phase 0 — approve direction.** Record an ADR (e.g. ADR-030 "Frontage combat") with this document as
the decision record. No code.

**Phase 1 — non-breaking correctness prep.** The unaffordable-candidate requeue fix shipped in v0.24
(ADR-031). Reserve pending attack commands in
`resolveOpposingClash` via `availableAttackOrders` instead of reading the raw array. Extract helpers
`getAttackStats(playerId, defenderId)` and the derived structure-stack cache. Add `combatRules` to
`SimulationOptions` and the snapshot. Tests: requeue invariant, pending-order reservation.

**Phase 2 — supply economy.** Add constants, `supply`/`supplyAccrual`/`holdTicks` state, accrual,
derived cap/income, checksum fields, HUD readout, and `no-supply` failure reason behind the flag.
Keep the old order path intact for `'stress'`. Tests: accrual exactness over 600 ticks vs a closed-form
expectation, cap clamping, determinism fixture.

**Phase 3 — frontage combat.** Replace `AttackState.orders/initialOrders/lifetimeTicks` with
`strength/power/effectiveLevels/logTier/idleTicks`. Launch cost/cap. Capture spends strength. Replace
`resolveOpposingClash` with the simultaneous attrition exchange. Implement expiry causes and failure
reasons. Rewrite the old clash acceptance tests; add invariant tests (strength never negative, no
capture without cost, front cap, no interpenetration, deterministic attrition order).

**Phase 4 — structures and bots.** Add `supply-income` and `supply-cap` effect kinds; update Command
Post and War Academy definitions, cards, and UI copy; retune bot build weights; make bots supply-gated
and strength-aware. Tests: economy effect mapping, bot determinism.

**Phase 5 — victory and pacing.** Add `holdTicks`, dominance victory, timeout score victory, result
screen end cause, and snapshot fields. Add headless pacing tests using a fixed seed set.

**Phase 6 — validation and docs.** Run the telemetry gates (§6), tune only the constants marked
tunable, then update [SPEC.md](SPEC.md), [EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md) (archive EXP-001),
[DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md), [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md), [DETERMINISM.md](DETERMINISM.md),
[NETWORKING.md](NETWORKING.md), [PERFORMANCE.md](PERFORMANCE.md), [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md), and the
invariants in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md). Retire the attack-stress UI copy; keep the stress mode as a
benchmark switch.

Files expected to change: `constants.ts`, `simulation.ts`, `Structures/{types,definitions,
command-post,war-academy}.ts`, `net/protocol.ts`, `+page.svelte`, `GameCanvas.svelte` if front markers
change, `simulation.test.ts`, `economy.test.ts`, `scripts/benchmark.ts`, and the docs above. No map,
RNG, renderer, or integrity rewrite is required.

## 6. Telemetry and validation plan

Keep simulation purity: counters are integers computed inside `step()` (no `Date`, no DOM) and either
derived by the headless harness from per-tick snapshots, or emitted as a `MatchSummary` that is not part
of the checksum. A new `scripts/combat-validation.ts` runs headless Node matches over a fixed seed set.

### 6.1 Metrics per match

- Metadata: seed, `mapVersion`, `protocolVersion`, `combatRules`/`combatRulesVersion`, player count,
  tick rate.
- Outcome: final tick, winner, end cause, final land shares, hold ticks, eliminations timeline.
- Pacing: time to first hostile contact, time to first elimination, time to first 50% leader, global
  capture rate (land cells/s over 60-tick windows), leader land share over time, matches ending by
  timeout.
- Combat: launches per player per minute, supply earned/spent/left at end, max concurrent fronts,
  front death causes (strength/idle/lifetime/elimination/frontier), median front lifetime, median
  captures per front, attrition exchanges per match, capture cost paid (total and per terrain tier),
  fronts stalled by defense for >100 ticks.
- Balance: win rate by seat/player id, human win rate against bots, card build counts, structure
  survival time, supply cap/income hit rates, average supply spent on Barracks vs economy.

### 6.2 A/B harness

Run the same seeds and bot profiles under `'stress'` and `'frontage'`:

- 40 seeds × 20 simulated minutes, all-bot, identical command logs where possible.
- Compare global capture rate, fronts alive over time, leader-share slope, and saturation time.
- A/B the proposed constant sets (income divisor, launch cost) before touching bot AI.

### 6.3 Acceptance gates before the default flips

| Gate          | Requirement                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Determinism   | Two identical runs of 20 seeds × 9 000 ticks produce equal checksums at 100-tick checkpoints; the same holds across a browser run and a Node run.                                                                                           |
| Invariants    | Supply never negative or above cap; strength never negative; fronts never exceed the cap; no capture without paying its cost; no cell captured without attacker adjacency; claimedBy/provenance consistent; land totals equal owner counts. |

## 7. Risks and mitigations

| Risk                                           | Why it matters                                                     | Mitigation                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Supply snowball                                | Territory → income → more fronts is self-reinforcing.              | No hard income/stock caps; tune territory divisors with telemetry and use front pressure/attrition.                |
| Two-resource complexity                        | Gold + supply adds HUD and decision load.                          | Keep the readout compact; supply is only launches; open decision to merge later.                                   |
| Front churn / click fatigue                    | More launches than the current 3-order tempo.                      | Front cap 8, early supply scarcity, long front lifetime, no reinforcement spam; revisit with playtests.            |
| Symmetric attrition feels like nothing happens | Both fronts may die together.                                      | Barracks advantage is real (strength + damage); terrain decides the rest; tune `CLASH_ATTRITION_*` from telemetry. |
| Unbreakable defensive walls                    | State aura + stacked Fortifications can outlast finite strength.   | Pierce, strength scaling, optional Fortification stack cap; telemetry counter "stalled fronts".                    |
| Bot quality regression                         | Bots currently reason about attack orders; supply/strength is new. | Phase 4 retune, deterministic strength estimate, A/B win rates; keep bot decisions hash-based.                     |
| Perf under more fronts                         | Up to 8 per player vs current order caps.                          | Stress mode keeps a lower cap; measure with the 51-state benchmark; pair-once-per-tick attrition.                  |
| Protocol/checksum break                        | Snapshot, replay, and wire change.                                 | `COMBAT_RULES_VERSION`, protocol bump, dual fixtures for stress/frontage, staged flag.                             |
| Timeout decides too many games                 | A 60% hold may be hard to reach.                                   | Tune hold share/ticks; publish end cause; adjust only after 40-seed data.                                          |
| Stale docs/ADRs                                | Several ADRs already contradict current behavior (Appendix B).     | Phase 6 doc pass; mark superseded ADRs explicitly.                                                                 |
| Mobile/tap tempo                               | More frequent launches on small screens.                           | Compact supply readout, tap-to-launch with clear markers, front cap; mobile parity is Phase 1 of ROADMAP.          |

## Appendix B — Inconsistencies found in current docs

These were found while writing this proposal and should be fixed during Phase 6 (or earlier for the code
item). They are documentation defects, not proposed changes.

1. **ADR-014 is stale on the State aura.** It says radius **12** and that the aura applies to all land
   in range "regardless of owner". Current code and SPEC say radius **16** and friendly-owned land only
   (ADR-021). ADR-014's status note only mentions rendering supersession.
2. **ADR-011 contradicts ADR-024.** ADR-011 still says mutual fronts "mark every order between the two
   states done" and never capture the cell. The implemented rule is order-stripping attrition
   (ADR-024) that can break through. ADR-011 is marked superseded only for the mainland rule.
3. **ADR-016 contradicts ADR-022 on Command Posts.** ADR-016 says +1 order at 60-second global
   boundaries; ADR-022 and the code use continuous accrual. ADR-016 is not marked superseded for that
   bullet.
4. **Unaffordable-candidate requeue — resolved in v0.24 (ADR-031).** The proposal was written against
   a tree where `simulation.ts` pushed back every sampled cell except the chosen one, then broke when
   `chosenCost > attackPoints`; the cheapest cell was dropped and a front with no capture could die.
   The code now requeues all sampled cells, marks the tick cost-blocked, and keeps the front alive.
5. **ROADMAP Phase 0.5 is stale.** It says "Removed troops, income, caps, launch costs, and capture
   costs", but the current build has gold income, attack-order caps, and elevation-based capture costs.
6. **"Free attacks" wording.** AGENTS.md status and PERFORMANCE.md call the attacks free while the
   same documents say each launch costs an attack order and captures cost defense-derived points.
   "Resource-free" is accurate; "free" is not.
7. **Capture-cost descriptions omit aura and piercing.** ARCHITECTURE §3.5 says a capture spends
   `1 + elevation` (+1 occupied), and DETERMINISM §6 says `1 + elevation + occupied`; neither mentions
   Fortification/State auras or Barracks log-tier piercing, which SPEC §4.13/4.16 and ADR-019/024 include.
8. **SPEC §4.6 says a zero-capture tick removes the front** without the clash-pause exception stated
   in §4.3 step 14 (and implemented). Minor wording conflict.
9. **SPEC §4.3 step 7 states fixed caps** ("human 3 / bots 2") without saying these are base caps that
   War Academies raise. SPEC §4.17 does say the cap rises, so this is only a local wording gap.
10. **DECISIONS numbering is broken.** ADR-017 is missing, and ADR-022 is placed after ADR-029
    (line 915) instead of between ADR-021 and ADR-023. Navigation/reference hazard.
